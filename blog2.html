Controlling Hindi Dialects through Internal Activations: A Study of Steering Vectors in Nemotron-4

📅 February 23, 2026 ⏱️ 12 min read 👤 Sarthak
Activation Engineering Hindi LLM Bhojpuri Dialect Steering Vectors

Introduction

To understand Steering Vectors, we have to move beyond the traditional "input-output" view of Large Language Models. Instead of treating the model as a text-in, text-out machine, we treat it as a high-dimensional state space where concepts like "formal," "informal," or specific "dialects" are just directions.

Activation Engineering is an exciting new frontier in AI safety and control. Unlike fine-tuning, which permanently changes a model's weights, activation engineering involves modifying the model's internal activations in real-time. By injecting a Steering Vector—a mathematical representation of a specific trait—we can force the model to adopt a certain persona or dialect without retraining it.

In this experiment, I moved from simple identity shifts (like the "Dog" persona) to a much more complex challenge: shifting the linguistic dialect of a Hindi model toward a Bhojpuri-influenced Hindi style.

The Dataset: The "Khan Sir" Factor

Finding a dataset for a specific regional dialect blend is difficult. For this experiment, I curated a custom dataset using transcripts from Khan Sir's videos. His teaching style is a perfect, authentic blend of standard Hindi and the Bhojpuri dialect. This provides a rich "Target" set of activations that carry the nuances of regional cadence and vocabulary that traditional datasets lack.

Working

The subtraction process used to isolate the Bhojpuri dialect vector.

As shown in the diagram, we isolate the dialect through subtraction: Steering Vector = Activations(Khan Sir) - Activations(Standard Hindi). By subtracting the standard Hindi activations from the Khan Sir dataset activations, we cancel out common features like "General Knowledge" or "Instruction Following," leaving behind a distilled vector representing the Bhojpuri dialectal influence.

Experimental Methodology & Timeline

This was not a "one-and-done" success. The experiment involved a rigorous timeline consisting of over 15 different trials. Each trial involved varying the layers of intervention and the steering coefficient ($\lambda$).

  • Early Trials (1-5): Targeted early layers (1-8). The model produced garbled tokens, as these layers are too focused on basic syntax to handle complex dialectal shifts.
  • Middle Trials (6-10): Targeted layers 12-20. The model showed signs of dialect adoption but often "self-corrected" back to standard Hindi by the end of the sentence.
  • Final Trials (11-15): Targeted deep layers (24, 26, 28) with a high coefficient ($\lambda \approx 40$). This successfully bypassed the model's inherent "Standard Hindi" instruction tuning.

Analysis and Current State

Current Performance: High Promise, Imperfect Output

It is important to note that the outputs are not yet perfect. Because the dataset was derived from only one source (Khan Sir) and is relatively small, the model sometimes struggles with "semantic drift"—where it starts in a Bhojpuri dialect but drifts into repetitive loops or loses the original prompt's context.

However, the results show immense promise. The model successfully adopts specific Bhojpuri sentence structures and vocabulary that were previously inaccessible through simple prompting alone.

Conclusion

Steering vectors and activation engineering represent a shift in how we "talk" to AI. Instead of asking it to behave a certain way, we are directly manipulating the mathematical representations of that behavior. While my current Hindi dialect experiment is limited by dataset size, it proves that we can effectively steer models into regional linguistic nuances using very specific, curated data sources.
